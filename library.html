Origins

The History of Ifa

Ifa is one of the oldest continuously practiced divination systems in the world, tracing its origins to Ile-Ife, the spiritual homeland of the Yoruba people in what is now southwestern Nigeria. At its center stands Orunmila, the Orisa of wisdom and destiny, said to have witnessed the creation of the world and to hold knowledge of every path a life can take. Where other traditions look to fixed texts, Ifa is carried through the Odu — 256 sacred verses memorized, interpreted and passed down by trained priests known as Babalawo, "fathers of the mysteries."

For centuries, becoming a Babalawo required years of apprenticeship: memorizing thousands of verses, learning the proverbs, stories and prescriptions attached to each Odu, and mastering the tools of divination — the Opele chain and the sixteen sacred Ikin nuts. A reading was never a simple fortune. It was a conversation between the seeker, the diviner, and the accumulated wisdom of generations, arriving at not just what a person's situation was, but what Ebo — offering or sacrifice — might restore balance to it.

The Atlantic slave trade scattered this knowledge across the ocean, and rather than disappearing, Ifa took root wherever Yoruba people were taken. In Cuba it grew into Santeria and the practice of Ifa remains active among Lucumi priests. In Brazil it shaped Candomble. In Trinidad and Haiti it wove into Orisa worship and Vodou. Today, Ifa is practiced not only across West Africa but throughout the diaspora — in the UK, the United States, Canada and beyond — carried forward by communities determined to keep the tradition intact even at great distance from Ile-Ife.

What Ifa Oracle continues is not a reinvention of this practice but an extension of its reach: the same 256 Odu, the same proverbs and prescriptions a Babalawo has always drawn on, made reachable to anyone seeking clarity — wherever in the world they happen to be standing.

The Corpus

The 256 Odu

An Odu is a sacred verse or chapter within Ifa — part proverb, part story, part prescription. Every reading is governed by one Odu, cast through the Opele or Ikin. There are 256 in total, built from combinations of 16 principal Odu, each carrying its own proverbs, stories and guidance. Below are the 16 principal Odu; the 240 combinations will be added progressively.

Eji Ogbe +
Light, clarity and the dawn of new understanding.
Considered the first and most senior of the Odu, governing clarity, leadership and beginnings made in full light rather than uncertainty.
Oyeku Meji +
Darkness, mystery and the wisdom held within endings.
Balances Eji Ogbe as its counterpart — associated with what is hidden, with death and rebirth, and with the patience required to move through darkness.
Iwori Meji +
Insight gained through observing what others overlook.
Linked to perception and the ability to see beneath the surface of a situation — often signaling that hidden knowledge is about to come to light.
Odi Meji +
Foundations, the womb, and what must be protected.
Associated with depth, containment and the unseen structures — physical or spiritual — that hold a life or a household together.
Irosun Meji +
Warning, and the need to honor what came before.
Often carries a message about ancestral obligation — attention owed to elders, lineage or past agreements that have gone unaddressed.
Owonrin Meji +
Sudden reversal, and change arriving without warning.
Governs instability and unpredictable turns of fortune — a call to remain grounded and adaptable when circumstances shift quickly.
Obara Meji +
Pride, self-worth and the danger of overreach.
Speaks to confidence and generosity, and the caution needed to keep both from tipping into arrogance or empty boasting.
Okanran Meji +
Urgency, conflict and the consequences of delay.
Often points to tension that has been avoided for too long, and the need to act or speak before a situation worsens.
Ogunda Meji +
Struggle, iron, and the strength forged through hardship.
Connected to Ogun, Orisa of iron and labor — signaling a path that clears itself through effort, courage and the willingness to fight for it.
Osa Meji +
Turbulence, and the unseen forces moving beneath a situation.
Associated with hidden opposition and the power of Aje — a call to spiritual protection and discernment about who can be trusted.
Ika Meji +
Betrayal, and the wound of a trust broken.
Frequently addresses harm caused by those close to a person, and the ritual work needed to heal or guard against it.
Oturupon Meji +
Reputation, secrecy and what is spoken in the dark.
Governs how a person's name is carried by others — warning against gossip and affirming the protection of one's good standing.
Otura Meji +
Truth-telling, and the peace that honesty restores.
Associated with resolving conflict through open speech, and with the healing that follows once the truth is finally spoken.
Irete Meji +
Perseverance, and rising again after a fall.
Speaks to resilience — a reminder that setbacks are not final, and that steady effort restores what was thought lost.
Ose Meji +
Celebration, sweetness and rewards well earned.
Linked to Oshun, Orisa of love and abundance — a season of blessing, though one that asks for gratitude in return.
Ofun Meji +
Purity, elder wisdom and the weight of sacred duty.
Considered among the most senior Odu — associated with old age, moral responsibility and the burden of carrying sacred knowledge well.
Foundations

Key Concepts

Ori

Your personal guardian soul and divine head — the inner force believed to guide destiny before birth and after.

Ebo

Offering or sacrifice, prescribed after a reading to restore balance — anything from kindness performed to items presented at a shrine.

Esu / Elegba

The gatekeeper and messenger between worlds — often misunderstood as a devil figure, but properly the keeper of crossroads and choice.

Ire & Ibi

The two currents every Odu carries — Ire, blessing and good fortune, and Ibi, challenge or misfortune to be guarded against.

The Orisa

Divine forces of nature and human experience — Oshun, Ogun, Sango and others — each governing a domain of life.

Babalawo

"Father of the mysteries" — the trained priest who memorizes the Odu and performs divination on behalf of others.

Opele & Ikin

The two primary tools of divination — a chain of eight half-seed shells, and a set of sixteen sacred palm nuts.

Eewo

Taboos — specific things a person is advised to avoid, tied to their Ori or their Odu, said to protect their path.

Yoruba Proverbs

A starting collection of authentic Yoruba Ifa proverbs. Expanding over time.

"Bi a ba n gun igi, a n wo eyi t'o le."
"When climbing a tree, one watches for the strong branch."
Meaning: choose your foundation with care before committing your weight to it.
"Ọwọ́ ọmọdé kò tó pẹpẹ, tàgbà kò wọ akèrègbè."
"A child's hand can't reach the high shelf; an elder's hand can't fit the small gourd."
Meaning: every generation has its own role, and neither can fully replace the other.
"Bí ọjọ́ bá ń lọ, ọgbọ́n a máa ń wọlé."
"As days pass, wisdom enters the house."
Meaning: understanding is built slowly, through lived time, not shortcuts.
"Ẹní tí a ò kíni kò lè ṣíkà."
"One who is never greeted cannot easily do harm."
Meaning: community and connection are part of what keeps a person accountable.
"Ìgbà tí a bá fi ọwọ́ kan iná, l'á mọ̀ ọ́n gbóná."
"It's when we touch the fire that we know it's hot."
Meaning: some lessons are only fully understood through direct experience.
"Ìwà rere l'ẹ̀ṣọ́ ènìyàn."
"Good character is a person's true adornment."
Meaning: conduct outlasts appearance as the measure of a person.
